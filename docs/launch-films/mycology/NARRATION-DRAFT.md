## M1. A question before dispatch

### M101
A harvest note tells you what came out of the room. A shipment record tells you where some of it went. The difficult question arrives when you need both at once. Which recipients are linked to this lot, how much was recorded for each, and where did the original lot information come from? The answer should not depend on someone remembering an ordinary day perfectly.

### M102
This is Example Farm Cedar, a fictional farm used to demonstrate the records. The names, dates, weights and events are invented. We will follow one harvest from a notebook entry into a reviewed lot, through two recorded shipments, and back through a correction. The point is not to show an unusually tidy operation. It is to keep the history understandable when an entry needs attention.

### M103
Crowe Logic Mycology is the workspace for that story. The work begins with the grower's own records and decisions. A harvest does not become more accurate because it appears in a new table. A document does not become a food-safety certification because someone approves it locally. What matters here is making the source, the review and the later changes visible enough that another person can follow them.

## M2. Give the record a home

### M201
The first piece is the facility context. Example Farm Cedar has a name and a local operator, so a saved action is attached to the example workspace instead of floating without an owner. I keep that identity consistent throughout the record trail. It describes who acted in this local profile. It is not a substitute for verifying a person's identity or for whatever review your own operation requires.

### M202
The harvest begins in the Cultivation notebook. The example entry says oyster mushrooms, Room A, twenty-four pounds, harvested on the eighteenth of September. Those details are the starting facts for this demonstration. They are not measurements taken by the app. I enter them as a grower would enter a note, then read them back before asking the compliance ledger to carry them forward.

### M203
The notebook and the ledger serve different purposes. One holds the working cultivation record. The other will preserve a reviewed lot and the events connected to it. Copying information between those roles is a decision worth seeing. If I simply assume that a note is already approved because it exists, I lose the moment when someone actually checked what the new lot was going to say.

### M204
So I begin with the source, not with the Save button. Is the date the date I intend? Is the room correct? Is the species description the one in the source? Does the weight use the right unit? These are ordinary questions, but they make the difference between carrying a record forward and carrying a mistake forward. The example stays small enough to read every field.

## M3. Carry the source forward

### M301
Adoption creates a separate lot from the reviewed harvest. I select the source and open the review form. The source details and the lot fields need to be understood together, because an adopted lot is not permission to rewrite the notebook behind it. I review the twenty-four-pound entry, confirm the intended details, and complete the adoption through the normal record controls.

### M302
After saving, the new lot has its own identity. Its source is still available with the preserved snapshot and local review information. That is the important connection: not merely that two rows happen to use similar words, but that the lot retains the source that was reviewed. The actual lot code comes from the application. I use that code when following the record through the rest of the example.

### M303
The source snapshot and the current notebook entry are not the same kind of thing. A working note may later need an edit. The preserved snapshot answers a different question: what source was carried into this lot at the time of adoption? Keeping those apart allows a later reader to understand a difference rather than seeing the past silently change to match the newest version of a note.

### M304
The review attribution and time also have a specific purpose. They identify the local review that created this record. They do not make the source infallible, and they do not turn a local account into an outside inspector. They make the action explainable. If a question comes back later, the record should help you find the decision and the information available when that decision was made.

### M305
I return to the available harvest sources and check the adoption state. This source should not become a second lot just because the same button is used again. There is already a lot connected to it. That check protects the meaning of the record trail. Two lots with different codes would look like two separate harvests to someone who did not know they came from the same entry.

## M4. Record the day honestly

### M401
A lot is not the whole working day. The next entry belongs to Room A on the following date. It contains an example temperature of sixty-four degrees Fahrenheit and relative humidity of eighty-eight percent. These are fictional readings, not recommended growing targets. The useful part is that a reading belongs to a date and a room, with its context intact when someone returns to it later.

### M402
The example also has a missing second entry on the room check sheet. I record that discrepancy and leave cleaning marked incomplete. There is no reason to make the form look finished when the underlying record is not. An open deviation tells the next person that something still needs attention. It is more useful than a reassuring note that hides the question inside a paragraph of routine work.

### M403
Writing a corrective action and resolving a deviation are separate steps. I can describe what needs to happen while the issue remains open. In this example, the operator reviews the missing entry and records the follow-up. The wording should explain the action that was taken in the fictional exercise, not quietly transform an incomplete check into a claim that every condition in the room was acceptable.

### M404
Only then do I use the explicit resolution action. The original deviation stays part of the record, alongside the corrective action and resolution details. That is the useful difference between closing a task and erasing its history. If a later review asks why the entry was incomplete, the answer can still begin with the original note instead of a clean form that no longer explains what happened.

### M405
The daily log gives the lot context without pretending to measure more than was entered. A checkbox cannot establish that a cleaning process was effective. A pair of readings cannot describe an entire day. But an honest, dated entry can make the limits visible and give a reviewer something concrete to follow up. The record becomes useful by being specific, not by sounding more certain than the work supports.

## M5. Review the instruction

### M501
The example needs a short instruction for checking a lot label. I create a document called Example lot-label check. Its first revision asks the operator to compare the lot code with the selected record and note a mismatch before proceeding. It is a demonstration document, not a complete operating procedure for a real facility. The point is to follow the instruction through its review states.

### M502
A draft is a place to work on wording. Submission means the revision is ready for local review. Approval records the local owner's decision about that specific revision. Those three moments should not be collapsed into one. The words may look identical from one moment to the next, but the responsibility attached to them has changed. A reader needs to know which state the document is actually in.

### M503
I submit the first revision, review its text and approve it locally. Now there is an approved version to refer to. The approval belongs to that content, not to every future edit someone might want to make. That is why the next change begins as a new draft revision instead of quietly replacing the words that were approved before. The earlier decision remains tied to the earlier text.

### M504
For revision two, I add a check of the recorded harvest date as well as the lot code. While that revision is still a draft, the earlier approved instruction remains the active approved version. The new wording can be considered without pretending it was already in effect. That is a practical distinction whenever someone is working from an instruction while another person is preparing an improvement.

### M505
After the new revision is submitted and approved, the record shows which version it supersedes. I can read the current instruction and still find the older one. That is the outcome we need from this part of the example: a clear current document and a clear history of how it got there. Local approval is a recorded workflow decision, not an official certification or a regulatory electronic signature.

## M6. Account for the allocation

### M601
Now the lot connects to recipients. Example Kitchen One and Example Market Two are fictional customers without real contact details. I create their records before entering the shipments. A recipient name is part of the trace, but the shipment also needs to preserve the recipient details as they were when that entry was made. Later changes to a customer record should not rewrite the meaning of an earlier shipment.

### M602
The first allocation is twelve pounds from the twenty-four-pound lot to Example Kitchen One. I check the recipient, lot, amount and example shipment time before recording it. Then I read the saved result. The action is recording a shipment, not proving that a vehicle left, a scale was calibrated or a product was cleared for sale. The physical operation remains the grower's responsibility.

### M603
The second allocation is eight pounds to Example Market Two. Together the two recorded shipments account for twenty pounds. Four pounds remain unallocated in this lot record. That balance should be visible alongside the shipment entries, not reconstructed from memory. The numbers are simple on purpose. They let us follow the same quantity through the lot, the two recipients and the remaining balance without hiding behind a summary.

### M604
A proposed allocation larger than the remaining recorded amount needs to stop rather than make the balance negative. In a rehearsal, that is a useful check to perform with fictional data. In ordinary work, the important habit is to read a rejection as a reason to inspect the entry, not as permission to keep pressing Submit. A different number is a different request and needs its own review.

### M605
There is another state that deserves care: no clear result after a shipment was sent to be saved. Absence from the displayed list does not prove nothing happened. Keep the original request and follow the recovery instructions instead of entering the shipment again as new work. A trace is only helpful if uncertainty does not quietly become a duplicate allocation. This example's completed entries must be reconciled before we move them elsewhere.

## M7. Follow the lot

### M701
The question from the beginning is now answerable within the records. I open the lot's recall view and follow its recorded allocations. Example Kitchen One is linked to twelve pounds. Example Market Two is linked to eight. The total is twenty. This is a trace list for the selected lot. It does not contact the recipients or complete an external recall on the grower's behalf.

### M702
The recipient and lot facts retained with each shipment matter here. A trace should describe what was recorded at shipment time, not just whatever happens to be in the current customer or lot form. That makes it possible to compare the historical entry with a later correction. Without that distinction, a record could look consistent today only because yesterday's details had been silently replaced.

### M703
Here is the correction in our fictional exercise. The original total included a duplicate half-pound entry. The reviewed harvest total should be twenty-three and a half pounds, not twenty-four. I append a correction with that reason. This is a correction to the recorded harvest quantity. It is not a return, a spoilage entry or an explanation of where any physical product went after dispatch.

### M704
The two recorded shipments still total twenty pounds. The current remaining balance is now three and a half. The original entry and the correction history remain available, and the shipment-time lot snapshots keep the facts they carried before this correction. That lets a reviewer see both answers: what the record said when the shipment was entered, and what the current corrected lot says now.

## M8. Keep the history through recovery

### M801
A useful record trail also needs a way to survive an orderly move. For this example I preserve the source and export the selected notebook and compliance ledger separately. They are independent copies, not a single live synchronization process. I pause source work while preparing them, so the two exports are not casually taken from different moments in a changing day and then described as one transaction.

### M802
The destination begins empty. The notebook import has its own review and confirmation. The compliance restore has another. A successful notebook import does not tell me that the ledger restored, and a restored ledger does not prove the notebook arrived. I check each outcome in its own place. If one succeeds and the other does not, I preserve the success and investigate the other result rather than starting everything over.

### M803
The confirmations are deliberate: import an empty notebook, restore an empty farm. Existing destination records are not disposable obstacles to a cleaner demonstration. I do not erase them to force a restore through. The checksum helps detect a damaged copy, but it is not proof of who created the file. The actual records and their retained relationships still need to be inspected after the operation.

### M804
After an orderly restart, I return to the adopted lot. The source evidence and original review should remain connected. The two shipment recipients and amounts should still be present. The original harvest quantity and its correction should still be distinguishable. The ledger's earlier history should remain, with the restore receipt added. Those are the checks that make the recovered example useful, rather than merely showing that a file was accepted.

### M805
The source has not been deleted or retired by this process. Source and destination are independent copies, so continuing ordinary work in both would let them diverge. After verifying a real transfer, the grower needs to choose which copy is operational. Here we keep both as fictional demonstration evidence. We have shown an orderly copy and restart, not a power-loss experiment or a promise that every kind of failure is recoverable.
