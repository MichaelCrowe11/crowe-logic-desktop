## Proposal 1 · verified · proposed by crowelm-zenith

Step 2: Make cross-device continuity a replicated view of one authoritative Room journal, not replication of executable authority.

Evidence and rationale: rooms/engine.js already serializes messages, roster/model pins, costs, routines, council and councilHistory through toSession/fromSession; fromSession pauses saved ready or active councils. rooms/council.js persists phase events and proposals and refuses to run anything except a newly ready grant. rooms/council-host.js keeps execution ownership in an in-memory runs Map, which is not a cross-device ownership mechanism. docs/COUNCIL-AUTOPILOT.md explicitly describes separate desktop session storage, browser-local storage and mobile Preferences, with no synchronization. companion.js provides device authentication and remote command routes, but no Room synchronization protocol. These facts support adding durable continuity without treating a copied session or paired device as permission to execute.

Decision: Give each synchronized Room one authoritative host and a durable, monotonically ordered journal. Desktop, web and mobile render authenticated projections of that journal. Clients submit explicit commands to the owner rather than merge mutable Room snapshots. Initially pin the owner to one configured host; do not add automatic failover or ownership transfer. Losing that host permits cached viewing, not another runner starting the same grant. This deliberately chooses safety over offline execution availability and builds on the approved Step 1 dispatch boundary without re-proposing it.

Data and interfaces: Introduce a versioned Room checkpoint containing roomId, ownerId, ownerEpoch, journalSeq and the existing serialized Room state. Journal entries contain eventId, roomId, ownerEpoch, journalSeq, type, runId where applicable, and references to the relevant proposal, evidence and receipt. Keep authorityRevision separate from journalSeq: transcript updates are not grants. Store proposal content, frozen evidence, ballots and execution receipts durably on the owner, with authenticated, access-controlled projections for clients. Credentials and live grant capabilities never enter synchronized state; historical grant fields are evidence only. Reading workspace snapshots on another device requires explicit data-access permission, not merely pairing or Room membership, and redacted projections identify omitted evidence rather than implying it was checked.

Define readRoom(roomId, afterSeq) for a checkpoint plus ordered deltas, and submitRoomCommand({roomId, commandId, expectedRevision, ownerEpoch, operation, payload}) for explicitly initiated commands. Authenticate the caller at the receiver and resolve permissions through Step 1. The owner assigns sequence numbers and canonical message identities. Atomically check the expected revision, record command acceptance and apply the associated journal mutation. Persist commandId plus a payload digest and outcome: an identical duplicate returns its recorded status, while reuse with different content is rejected. A stale command returns a conflict and requires review, not a silent merge. Clients detect sequence gaps and reload a checkpoint; duplicate or reordered updates must not duplicate messages, votes, answered questions or routine claims. Retain deduplication records for the accepted epoch and reject commands from retired epochs.

Execution lifecycle: Link Step 1 actionId to a durable dispatch record containing runId, grantId, ownerEpoch, authorityRevision, proposal/evidence digest and outcome state. Before an effect, durably mark dispatch as started and recheck current authority at the resource boundary. A started action without a terminal receipt after interruption is outcome-unknown, never eligible for automatic redispatch. An identical retransmitted command can query that record but cannot restart its effect. Preserve the existing saved pre-write snapshot and partial-write escalation in rooms/council-files.js; a journal cannot make its multi-file writes transactional. Store final outcomes before acknowledging completion. Storage failure before the durable dispatch marker prevents execution; failure after an effect leaves an unresolved record requiring operator review.

Recovery and device semantics: A client disconnect does not restart, pause or clone a still-running owner process. Reconnection reads its current state. Owner restart or recovery pauses every nonterminal council, including ready, and requires a new explicit grant after receipt review, preserving the current no-replay contract. A persisted owner epoch changes on recovery, and all execution boundaries reject stale epochs; the owner store must enforce exclusive writer ownership before any runner starts. Do not release a second owner merely because the first is unreachable. Unresolved actions remain flagged across new grants and cannot be automatically carried forward as pending work. Only the owner claims routine occurrences, keyed by routineId and scheduledFor, retaining the existing claim-before-call behavior rather than allowing every synchronized client to schedule the same occurrence. Offline action commands are not queued for automatic execution on reconnect. Offline pause/revoke controls must say delivery is unconfirmed until the owner durably acknowledges them.

Safety invariants: Synchronization never creates a ready grant, imports a live capability, extends expiry, upgrades a tier or adds council execution modes. Remote commands still require receiver-side authorization, and desktop workspace authority remains local to the authorized executor. Quorum and replicated ballots are evidence, not authority. Revocation prevents subsequent dispatch after it is accepted at the owner, but cannot undo committed effects. A device can continue viewing the same Room without inheriting another device's permission to act. A checksum supports integrity checking, not authentication of a client-supplied grant or journal.

Verification and acceptance: Add proposed tests around an injected durable store and fake client connections: desktop/web/mobile converge on the same journal and council history; reordered and duplicate deltas converge without duplicate messages; simultaneous answers to one question admit one decision; stale revisions, forged epochs and conflicting commandId payloads are rejected; two clients cannot claim one routine occurrence twice. Inject crashes before dispatch persistence, after persistence but before the effect, after the effect but before the receipt, and after receipt persistence but before acknowledgment. Require no automatic action redispatch in every interruption case and visible outcome-unknown status where evidence is incomplete. Exercise owner restart, concurrent owner startup, revoked devices, disconnected revocation, omitted evidence and storage failures. Re-run existing grant, stale-snapshot, scope and per-write authority checks. These are acceptance tests to implement, not observed results.

First implementation touchpoints and limits: Start with rooms/engine.js for versioned persistence and rooms/council.js for durable run/action references and recovery semantics; then rooms/council-host.js for exclusive ownership and journal-backed command handling, companion.js for authenticated continuity transport, and docs/COUNCIL-AUTOPILOT.md for device behavior. The document names renderer/rooms-local.js as a shared advisory adapter, but its implementation and the underlying stores are not supplied, so their transaction and transport capabilities are not established. This step specifies required durability semantics rather than claiming the current stores provide them. It does not provide automatic host migration, offline execution, exactly-once external effects, hostile-process isolation or strict dollar accounting. The next bounded step is atomic spend reservation and real-cost settlement using this single authoritative owner.
